Accessibility statement

Last updated: 14 October 2026

Edgepost should work for everyone, whatever the device, the browser or the assistive technology. This statement covers this website and says what we checked, what we know falls short, and how to tell us.

Scope

This statement covers the pages at edgepost.dev: the home page, the privacy policy, the legal notice and this page. The app follows the accessibility features of iOS, iPadOS and Android: text that grows with the system text size, a spoken label on every control for VoiceOver and TalkBack, touch targets of at least 44 points, Reduce Motion, and light and dark appearances designed as equals.

Standard and conformance status

We aim for the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG 2.2) at level AA. This is a self-assessment, not an audit by a third party; the site has not been tested by people who use assistive technology day to day.

What we have done

  • Text and controls meet a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 in both the light and the dark palette, which follow your system setting unless you pick one with the theme switch at the top of each page.
  • Everything works with a keyboard: a skip link, a visible focus ring, the theme switch (Escape closes it), the tour's chapter buttons, the device tabs (arrow keys move between them) and the screenshot viewer (arrow keys page through it, Escape closes it).
  • Every page reads in order without its styles, uses one heading outline, and names its landmarks.
  • Every screenshot has a text description of what it shows.
  • Pages reflow to 320 pixels wide with no sideways scrolling, and text can be enlarged to 200% without loss.
  • When your system asks for reduced motion, the tour swaps screens with a plain fade and nothing slides or rises.
  • The site works without JavaScript: the tour shows each screen under its step, the screenshots stay in their lists, and the theme follows your system setting.

Known limitations

  • The screenshots show the app in English, with demo data; they are pictures of the screens, described in text, not the screens themselves.
  • The licence excerpt is set in a monospaced face as it appears in the source.

Technologies relied on

HTML, CSS, SVG and, for the tour, the tabs, the viewer and the theme switch, JavaScript.

How this statement was prepared

By reviewing the pages against WCAG 2.2 level AA: contrast measured for every text and control colour in both palettes, keyboard-only use, the pages read at 320 pixels wide, and the heading and landmark structure checked.

Feedback and contact

If something on this site or in the app does not work for you, email contact@edgepost.dev or open an issue on GitHub. Say which page or screen, and what you were trying to do; we aim to answer within a week.
